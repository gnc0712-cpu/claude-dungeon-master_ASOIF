# The World of Ice and Fire

This campaign runs on **D&D 5e mechanics** in an **original ASOIAF-flavored setting**: your own corner of Planetos, your own era, your own Houses. It is not a retelling of the books or show — treat named canon events (Robert's Rebellion, the War of the Five Kings, the Dance of the Dragons, etc.) as distant history or legend the party has *heard of*, not things they're about to walk into, unless the player explicitly asks to play through one.

## Session Zero: Placing the Campaign

Before the first session, establish these with the player (ask directly if starting fresh):

1. **When**: Pick or invent an era relative to known history — e.g. "two generations before the Targaryen conquest," "a hundred years after some unnamed war," "deep in the Age of Heroes," or a wholly invented year with no fixed anchor to canon at all. Once picked, keep a simple in-world calendar (season + year) in `state.md`.
2. **Where**: Which region is home base? (See Regions below.) Most campaigns are stronger starting small — one region, one House, one town — and expanding outward.
3. **Season**: Westeros' seasons last years, not months, and are unpredictable. Decide what season the campaign opens in — it colors everything (a long summer breeds complacency and surplus; a long winter breeds desperation and want). Track season changes as a slow background clock.
4. **The Party's Place**: Are they a minor House's children and retainers? A sellsword company? Night's Watch recruits? Faceless Men novices? A hedge knight's retinue? This determines their starting resources and who they answer to. See `dm-instructions/campaign-generation.md` for ready-made campaign frames.
5. **Invent, Don't Import**: Create original Houses, lords, and local conflicts rather than reusing named canon Houses as major characters. It's fine to *reference* the great Houses (Stark, Lannister, Targaryen, etc.) as distant, rarely-seen powers whose banners and reputations shape the world — just don't make the party's story depend on impersonating or contradicting specific canon plot.

## Geography

### Westeros (west continent, roughly north to south)

| Region | Character | Great House (background reference only) |
|---|---|---|
| **Beyond the Wall** | Frozen wilds, Free Folk clans, the Night's Watch's ancient enemy territory, old magic | — (no lords; Free Folk clans) |
| **The Wall & Night's Watch** | 700-foot ice wall, the Watch's castles (Black, Shadow Tower, Eastwatch), oath-bound brothers | The Night's Watch (sworn brotherhood, not a House) |
| **The North** | Vast, cold, sparsely settled, Old Gods and weirwood godswoods, clannish honor culture | House Stark of Winterfell |
| **The Iron Islands** | Rocky, salt-battered isles, reaving culture, the Drowned God | House Greyjoy of Pyke |
| **The Riverlands** | Fertile, crossed by three great rivers, no natural defenses, fought over constantly | House Tully of Riverrun |
| **The Vale** | Mountain-locked, the Eyrie perched on a peak, insular highborn culture | House Arryn of the Eyrie |
| **The Westerlands** | Gold mines, wealth, ambition, Casterly Rock | House Lannister of Casterly Rock |
| **The Crownlands** | Around the capital (invent your own seat if avoiding the canon capital), politically dense | Whichever House holds the throne in your era |
| **The Reach** | Breadbasket of the realm, roses and knighthood, Highgarden | House Tyrell of Highgarden (or your own era's equivalent) |
| **The Stormlands** | Storm-battered coast, martial culture | House Baratheon of Storm's End (or your own era's equivalent) |
| **Dorne** | Desert and oasis, Rhoynar-blooded, different law (equal inheritance, paramours), poison lore | House Martell of Sunspear |

Feel free to swap out whichever House sits on a given seat in your era — the geography and culture of the region are the durable part; the ruling family is yours to invent.

### Essos (east continent) — reachable by ship for travel/trade/exile arcs

- **Braavos**: Secretive canal city founded by escaped slaves, home to the Iron Bank and the faceless order of assassin-priests. No lords; ruled by a Sealord and powerful guilds.
- **The Free Cities** (Pentos, Myr, Tyrosh, Lys, Volantis, Norvos, Qohor, Lorath): Mercantile city-states, each with a distinct character — Myr for lace and crossbows, Lys for pleasure houses, Volantis for old Valyrian pride and slavery, Qohor for smiths and sorcerers.
- **The Dothraki Sea**: Endless grass plains, horse-lord khalasars, no cities, no farming — strength and horsemanship rule.
- **Slaver's Bay**: Slave cities (Astapor, Yunkai, Meereen) built on the old Ghiscari empire, brutal and wealthy.
- **Qarth**: Fabulously wealthy trade city at the edge of the known world, strange warlocks and stranger customs.
- **The Shadow Lands / Asshai**: Far east, barely explored, the deepest and most dangerous source of true sorcery in the world.

## Cultures of Playable Characters

See `dm-instructions/character-sheets.md` for the mechanical "culture of origin" traits (replacing D&D race). Cultures include First Men, Andals, Ironborn, Rhoynar/Dornish, Free Folk, Valyrian-descended, Crannogmen, and several Essosi peoples (Braavosi, Dothraki, Summer Islander).

## Faiths

Religion is a real social force — belief, ritual, and taboo, not usually a source of spells for ordinary people. Player clerics/paladins are, again, the exception (see `spellcasting.md`).

- **The Old Gods**: Faceless, nameless gods of the First Men, worshipped at weirwood heart trees in godswoods. No temples, no priesthood, no holy text — oaths sworn before a heart tree are considered unbreakable. Strongest in the North and among the Free Folk.
- **The Faith of the Seven**: The dominant Andal religion — one god, seven aspects (Father, Mother, Warrior, Smith, Maiden, Crone, Stranger). Septs, septons/septas, the Faith Militant in troubled eras. Dominant across most of southern Westeros.
- **The Drowned God**: Ironborn faith — "what is dead may never die." Baptism by near-drowning, a violent and fatalistic theology ("we do not sow"). Rivaled internally by the more peaceful "Old Way" skepticism of raiding.
- **R'hllor, the Lord of Light**: Essosi in origin, a dualistic fire religion (R'hllor vs. the Great Other) with genuinely potent, showy magic among its priesthood — prophecy, fire-scrying, resurrection-adjacent miracles. Excellent source of a warlock patron or cleric domain for a party caster; genuinely alarming to bystanders.
- **The Many-Faced God**: Braavosi death cult underlying the Faceless Men — "valar morghulis," all men must die, all deaths are gifts of the same god wearing different faces.
- **Other Essosi faiths**: Ghiscari gods of Slaver's Bay, the Great Shepherd, various city gods of the Free Cities — invent freely, magic-flavor to taste.

## Tone Calibration

- **Low fantasy, high stakes**: Most people the party meets have never seen magic and don't believe in it. A single fireball is an era-defining event, not Tuesday.
- **Politics is the real dungeon**: Court scenes, marriage negotiations, oaths, and successions should get encounter-level prep — stakes, opposition, and consequences, same as a fight.
- **Winter is a countdown clock, not scenery**: If the campaign runs long enough to touch a season change, let stores, prices, and desperation shift accordingly.
- **No plot armor**: Named NPCs — allied or enemy — can die. So can PCs. Let the dice and choices stand.
- **Bastards carry their origin**: A highborn's bastard child takes a regional bastard surname instead of the father's name — Snow (North), Stone (Vale), Sand (Dorne), Storm (Stormlands), Rivers (Riverlands), Hill (Westerlands), Flowers (Reach), Pyke (Iron Islands), Waters (Crownlands). Useful worldbuilding hook for backstories and NPCs alike.
