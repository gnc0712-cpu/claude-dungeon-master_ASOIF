# Coin, Steel & Glass (Items & Loot)

Reference: `dnd-5e-srd/markdown/04 equipment.md`, `dnd-5e-srd/markdown/10 magic items.md`

Values and mechanics are unchanged from the SRD — only names and flavor shift. Convert gp/sp/cp directly to Westerosi coinage 1:1.

## Treasure Generation

### By Encounter CR

| CR | Coin Value | Notable Items |
|----|------------|-------------|
| 0-4 | 10-100 gold dragons | Consumables, common gear, a House token worth a small favor |
| 5-10 | 100-1,000 gold dragons | Dragonglass tools/weapons, fine steel, uncommon items |
| 11-16 | 1,000-10,000 gold dragons | Valyrian steel pieces, rare items, letters of marque or writs of authority |
| 17+ | 10,000+ gold dragons | Legendary artifacts, dragon eggs, land and title |

This is a guideline. Meaningful treasure should be earned, not expected — and in a low-magic world, *political* treasure (a writ, a betrothal, a debt owed by the Iron Bank) is often worth more than gold.

### Treasure Types

**Individual Treasure** (carried by defeated enemies):
- Copper and silver for common raiders/guards
- Gold for household knights or better
- Small valuables: House signet rings, letters, jewelry, trade goods

**Hoard Treasure** (a lord's strongbox, a wrecked ship's hold, a hidden cache):
- Significant coin in mixed denominations
- Art objects, gems, House heirlooms
- Valyrian steel or dragonglass items appropriate to the challenge
- Plot-relevant items: sealed letters, family records, betrothal contracts, incriminating evidence

## Valyrian Steel & Dragonglass

The two signature "magic item" categories of this setting — use them in place of generic +N weapons for anything with real narrative weight.

### Valyrian Steel

Forged with a lost art from doomed Old Valyria — folded, rippling, ink-dark steel, lighter and sharper than any modern smith can match. **Extremely rare**: a handful of blades exist in a given generation, almost always ancestral House heirlooms, never simply bought.

- Mechanically: treat as a +1 weapon (or +2 for a truly legendary, campaign-defining blade), plus it never needs sharpening/maintenance and holds an edge indefinitely.
- Setting-significant: effective against wights and (per old legend) the creatures beyond the Wall — treat as bypassing resistance/immunity to nonmagical weapons for such creatures.
- Reforging an existing Valyrian steel blade (rather than finding a new one) is a much more plausible way for a PC to acquire one — gate it behind a real cost: another heirloom sacrificed, a debt to a master armorer, a political favor.

### Dragonglass (Obsidian)

Volcanic glass, especially plentiful on Dragonstone. Common enough for the party to plausibly carry as tools/weapons; historically a smallfolk material (cheap, doesn't rust) until its old purpose was rediscovered.

- Mechanically: a masterwork material for daggers, arrowheads, and spearheads — no inherent magical bonus, but like Valyrian steel it bypasses nonmagical-weapon resistance/immunity for wights and the creatures beyond the Wall.
- Shatters more easily than steel against armor — fine narrative reason for it to be a specialist/backup weapon rather than a knight's primary steel.

## Poisons & Exotic Substances

Dorne and the Free Cities (especially Lys and Qarth) are the setting's flavor-source for poison lore — use the SRD's poison rules/DCs and reskin the names:

- **The Long Farewell**: slow-acting, undetectable poison (SRD "insanity mist" or similar slow poison, reflavored)
- **Sweetsleep**: causes deep unconsciousness rather than death — useful for kidnap plots
- **Manticore venom / the Strangler**: fast, lethal, causes visible suffocation — a dramatic assassination tool
- **Tears of Lys**: colorless, tasteless, mimics natural illness — favored for court poisonings that must look accidental
- **Moon tea**: not a poison — an abortifacient/contraceptive herbal brew, common enough to be a background/roleplay detail rather than a combat item

Use existing SRD poison mechanics (DC, damage, condition) and apply whichever flavor fits the scene.

## Magic Item Distribution

### Rarity Guidelines (unchanged from SRD)

| Rarity | Character Level | Approximate Value |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Common | 1+ | 50-100 gold dragons |
| Uncommon | 1+ | 101-500 gold dragons |
| Rare | 5+ | 501-5,000 gold dragons |
| Very Rare | 11+ | 5,001-50,000 gold dragons |
| Legendary | 17+ | 50,001+ gold dragons |

### Distribution Philosophy

- **Earn, don't give**: Items should feel like rewards, and in this setting, political/heirloom weight matters as much as mechanical power.
- **Magic should feel foreign**: Reserve true magic items (as opposed to Valyrian steel/dragonglass) for Essosi origins (Qarth, Asshai), R'hllor's priesthood, or very old First Men/Children of the Forest relics — never mundane market goods.
- **Match the story**: An item from a red priest's temple should read as fire/prophecy-themed; a relic from beyond the Wall should read as cold, old, and wrong.
- **Consider the party**: Items should be usable by someone.
- **Vary types**: Mix weapons, armor, utility, consumables.

### Consumables

- **Milk of the poppy**: the setting's painkiller/anesthetic — reskin *Potion of Healing* as a milk-of-the-poppy draught for narrative consistency, or keep them distinct (poppy dulls pain but doesn't restore HP; a true healing draught is rarer and more magical).
- **Scrolls**: found in maesters' libraries, red temples, or on Essosi sorcerers — genuinely rare and suspicious to own.
- **Dragonglass ammunition**: available in reasonable quantities near Dragonstone or through Night's Watch supply lines; scarce and precious elsewhere.

## Attunement

From SRD rules:
- Maximum 3 attuned items per character
- Attunement requires short rest focused on item
- Some items don't require attunement
- Curse effects may prevent ending attunement

Track attuned items on character sheets.

## Custom Items

When creating custom items:

1. **Start with a concept**: What should this do?
2. **Compare to existing**: Find similar SRD items for balance
3. **Set rarity**: Based on power level
4. **Add flavor**: Name, appearance, House/regional history
5. **Consider drawbacks**: Curses or limitations add interest — an item tied to a hated House name is its own liability

### Custom Item Template

```markdown
## [Item Name]

*[Type], [Rarity] (requires attunement?)*

[Physical description]

[Mechanical effects]

[History/Lore — which House, faith, or region it comes from]
```

## Loot Narration

Don't just list items. Describe the discovery:

**Boring**: "You find 50 gold and a longsword."

**Better**: "Beneath the dead knight's ruined cloak, your fingers close around a leather purse — fifty gold dragons stamped with a stag you don't recognize. Propped against the cart wheel, half-forgotten, is a longsword in a battered scabbard. Westerlands make, by the lion worked into the pommel — someone will want this back, or want you dead for having it."

## Economy in the Dark World

- Wealth is relative. Smallfolk see copper stars; lords see gold dragons and land.
- True magic items can't simply be purchased almost anywhere in Westeros — Valyrian steel and dragonglass are the closest thing to a "magic market," and even those move through House connections, not shops.
- Merchants and innkeepers may refuse service based on House colors, accent, or reputation (see Honor/Renown/Infamy in `houses-and-honor.md`).
- The Iron Bank of Braavos (and lesser Free City banking houses) will lend to anyone who can repay — and collects debts from lords and paupers alike, eventually.
- Taxes, tithes to the Faith, and a lord's cut erode wealth; smuggling and the black market exist precisely because of this.
