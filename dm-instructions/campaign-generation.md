# Campaign Generation

Reference: `dm-instructions/setting-westeros.md`, `dm-instructions/houses-and-honor.md`

## Campaign Frames

Pick one at Session Zero — it determines starting resources, who the party answers to, and what kind of story naturally follows. See `dm-instructions/setting-westeros.md` for placing it in a region and era.

### The Sworn House
The party are children, retainers, or sworn swords of a minor House. Starting resources: a small holdfast, a handful of household guards, a liege lord to answer to. Natural arcs: succession disputes, border skirmishes, marriage alliances, proving the House's worth.

### The Sellsword Company
The party are hired blades, contracted for coin, loyal to no banner but their own captain (who might be a PC). Starting resources: weapons, armor, a reputation to build, no land. Natural arcs: contracts that turn morally complicated, rival companies, a patron who wants more than was agreed.

### The Night's Watch
The party are new recruits at a Watch castle. Starting resources: black wool and steel, brothers-in-arms, no coin, no family name that matters anymore. Natural arcs: rangings beyond the Wall, Free Folk diplomacy or war, the slow horror of what's really out there, the politics of a dying order everyone else has forgotten.

### The Faceless Order
The party are novices of a death-cult assassins' order (Braavos-style). Starting resources: training, a temple/order to answer to, no name of their own by design. Natural arcs: contracts with hidden strings, the cost of erasing the self, a crisis of faith in "many faces, one god."

### The Hedge Knight's Retinue
A landless knight (possibly a PC) and companions, wandering for tourneys, odd jobs, and a lord willing to grant land. Starting resources: a horse, arms and armor, very little coin. Natural arcs: tourney circuits, chance encounters with the great and powerful, the long climb toward a household of their own.

### The Smallfolk Band
The party start as nobody — village-born, refugees, or camp followers — thrown together by war or disaster. Starting resources: whatever they can carry. Natural arcs: survival, banditry vs. banding together, a slow rise in Renown from nothing (see `houses-and-honor.md`).

## Era Templates

Ready-made original eras to offer a player who doesn't want to invent one from scratch. All are original to this campaign — not canon events.

### The Long Truce
Two rival Houses in the same region have kept an uneasy peace for a generation, sealed by a marriage that never quite settled the grudge. The old wound is still open under the surface. Good for: political intrigue, star-crossed loyalties, a slow-building war the party might start, stop, or get caught in.

### The Hungry Winter
Winter has lasted three years and counting; stores are thinning, and desperation is turning neighbors on each other. Good for: survival stakes, banditry, a lord's failure (or success) to provide for his people, moral compromise.

### The Broken Wall
A Watch castle has gone dark — no ravens, no word — and something beyond the Wall is stirring in ways the Free Folk themselves are frightened of. Good for: horror pacing, the Watch's political neglect by the southern kingdoms, wilderness survival, a slow-burn threat escalation.

### The Kingsmoot Season
A regional power (Iron Islands-style, or invent an equivalent elsewhere) has no clear heir, and multiple claimants are gathering support. Good for: a compressed political campaign, faction alignment, a decisive climactic vote or battle.

### The Silent Sea
A Free City's trade fleet has gone missing, and the Iron Bank wants answers before it wants revenge. Good for: an Essos-spanning campaign, mystery/investigation pacing, mercantile intrigue instead of martial stakes.

## Adventure Hook Types

### The Patron
Someone hires the party for a job:
- A lord or lady with more coin than trusted swords
- A merchant house whose caravan or ship vanished
- A dying maester who needs something retrieved before he's gone
- A smuggler or crime lord with a grudge and coin to spend

### The Threat
Danger comes to the party or someone they care about:
- Their home holdfast attacked or threatened with siege
- An assassination attempt with mysterious backers
- A plague or blight with unnatural origin
- Word from beyond the Wall that no one in power wants to believe

### The Discovery
Something found leads to adventure:
- A map or ledger pointing to a lost hoard or a hidden crime
- A dying rider with an urgent, undelivered raven-message
- A Valyrian steel blade or dragonglass cache that changes the party's standing
- An unexpected inheritance — land, title, or debt

### The Wronged
Justice demands action:
- A false accusation requires clearing a name (possibly via trial by combat)
- A loved one taken hostage, fostered against their will, or imprisoned
- Betrayal by a trusted bannerman or sworn brother
- Guest right broken, and someone wants it answered

## Story Arc Structure

### Three-Act Framework

**Act 1: The Hook (Sessions 1-3)**
- Establish the status quo — House, region, season
- Introduce the inciting incident
- First taste of the larger threat (political, martial, or otherwise)
- Party commits to the adventure

**Act 2: Rising Stakes (Sessions 4-8)**
- Investigation, travel, and court maneuvering
- Complications and setbacks — a broken alliance, a lost battle, a betrayal
- Allies and enemies revealed among Houses and factions
- Personal stakes emerge for each character
- Midpoint revelation changes understanding

**Act 3: Confrontation (Sessions 9-12)**
- Path to the final challenge becomes clear
- Difficult choices with no good options — often a choice between honor and survival
- Final preparation: gathering banners, allies, or resources
- Climactic confrontation (battle, trial, duel, or reveal)
- Resolution and consequences — who holds the seat now, what the realm remembers

### Pacing Beats

Within each arc, alternate:
- **Action**: Combat, chases, escapes, sieges
- **Exploration**: Discovery, investigation, travel between seats/cities
- **Court/Roleplay**: NPC and House politics, feasts, negotiations, personal character moments

## Location Creation

### The Five Room Dungeon

Quick framework for any location — works equally well for a literal ruin or a castle under political siege:

1. **Entrance/Guardian**: Challenge to enter, sets the tone
2. **Puzzle/Roleplay**: Non-combat obstacle — often a courtier, steward, or locked door of etiquette
3. **Trick/Setback**: Things get worse, false victory, or complication
4. **Big Challenge**: Challenging combat encounter or high-stakes confrontation
5. **Reward/Revelation**: Treasure, a secret, and/or plot advancement

### Location Atmosphere

For each significant location, define:
- **Sense**: What do they see, hear, smell? (woodsmoke, salt air, cold stone, incense)
- **History**: Why does this place exist? What happened here?
- **Danger**: What threatens visitors — martial, political, or environmental?
- **Secret**: What isn't obvious at first glance?
- **Purpose**: Why would the party come here?

### Environmental Storytelling

Let the environment tell stories:
- A House sigil painted over, badly, on an old banner
- Names scratched into a Watch castle's cell walls
- A child's wooden sword in an abandoned holdfast
- Signs of a desperate last stand at a river crossing
- A feast hall's high table set for a lord who never returned

## Encounter Balancing

### By Party Level

| Party Level | Easy | Medium | Hard | Deadly |
|-------------|------|--------|------|--------|
| 1 | 25 | 50 | 75 | 100 |
| 2 | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 |
| 3 | 75 | 150 | 225 | 400 |
| 4 | 125 | 250 | 375 | 500 |
| 5 | 250 | 500 | 750 | 1,100 |
| 6-7 | 300 | 600 | 900 | 1,400 |
| 8-10 | 450 | 900 | 1,400 | 2,100 |

XP values are per character. Multiply monster XP by:
- 2 monsters: x1.5
- 3-6 monsters: x2
- 7-10 monsters: x2.5
- 11+ monsters: x3

### Encounter Philosophy

- **Most encounters should be Medium to Hard**: Easy is boring, Deadly risks TPK
- **Resource attrition matters**: Multiple encounters before rest
- **Terrain makes fights interesting**: Cover, elevation, hazards, castle chokepoints
- **Enemies fight smart**: Use tactics, retreat when losing, call for reinforcements/banners
- **Vary encounter types**: Mix combat, political/social, and environmental challenges — a hostile court is as much an "encounter" as a raiding party

## World-Building Guidelines

### Start Small

Begin with:
- One region and one House
- One nearby threat (martial, political, or supernatural)
- Three named NPCs
- One mystery
- One rival faction

Expand as needed, not before.

### The "Three Truths" Method

For any new element (House, faction, NPC), establish three truths:
1. One obvious/known thing
2. One thing discoverable through investigation
3. One secret that changes everything

### Connections

Everything should connect:
- The steward's missing brother rides with the raiders threatening the holdfast
- The maester's cure requires ingredients from the haunted ruin beyond the border
- The innkeeper owes a debt to the local crime lord and reports on strangers

### Living World

Create the illusion of a world that moves:
- Reference events happening elsewhere via raven, rumor, and traveling singers
- Houses and NPCs remember and react to party actions
- Consequences arrive from earlier decisions, sometimes seasons later
- Rumors spread (some accurate, some not) — good fuel for Renown/Infamy shifts

## Campaign Themes

### Dark, Political Fantasy Elements

For gritty ASOIAF-flavored campaigns:
- **Moral ambiguity**: No clear heroes or villains — every House believes its own cause is just
- **Scarcity**: Resources, land, and legitimacy matter; nothing is free
- **Corruption**: Power corrupts, oaths get broken, institutions fail their people
- **Loss**: Not everyone survives; sacrifices are required, and named allies can die
- **Humanity**: Glimmers of hope and honor amid the scheming

### Questions to Drive Story

Each campaign should ask:
- What is worth dying for — a person, a House, an oath?
- How far will you go to protect your name and your kin?
- What do you become when duty and desire pull opposite ways?
- Who can truly be trusted when everyone answers to someone else?

## Session Zero Considerations

Before campaign start, establish:
- Era, region, and season (see `dm-instructions/setting-westeros.md`)
- Campaign frame (see above) and character connections within it
- Tone and content expectations
- Player goals (what do they want from the game?)
- Lines and veils (content to avoid/handle carefully — ASOIAF source material touches on war, violence, and morally dark themes; agree on limits up front)
- Scheduling and commitment
